Paul Graham reading notes · Commentary

Schlep Blindness

A “schlep” is a tedious, unglamorous task. The argument is that we overlook valuable problems precisely because the work involved looks unpleasant.

An independent interpretation written from my own compiled archive, not a reproduction of the original essay.

01

The central idea

We filter opportunities twice without noticing. First we ask whether a problem is important. Then, quietly, we ask whether the work would be dull — and the second filter often does the deciding.

For research this is a sharp question: which important problems do I dismiss because the integration, the data cleaning, the documentation or the coordination looks tedious?

02

The payments example

Stripe is the standard example: the difficult, unglamorous work of payment integration turned out to be worth doing. The lesson is not that every unpleasant task makes a good business. It is narrower and more useful — how tedious a problem is and how much it matters are two separate measurements, and we tend to collapse them into one.

03

Applying it to systems work

The version of this I actually run on my own work.

  1. List the problems users or developers keep complaining about.
  2. Score each one twice, separately: how much it matters, and how unpleasant the work would be.
  3. Look only at the high-value, high-tedium corner. That is where the overlooked work sits.
  4. Pick one and do the smallest useful version of it.
