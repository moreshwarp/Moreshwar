Schlep Blindness

Reading notes based on the supplied PDF, pages 3–6. This is an independent interpretation, not the original essay.

The central idea

A “schlep” is a difficult or tedious task. The archive argues that people may overlook worthwhile opportunities because the required work is unpleasant. This is a useful question for software research: which important problems do we dismiss because the implementation, integration, documentation, or coordination looks tedious?

Example: payment infrastructure

The PDF uses Stripe as an example of a company that addressed difficult payment integration work. The broader lesson is not that every unpleasant task creates a good business. It is that the difficulty of a problem should be evaluated separately from its importance and potential benefit.

Apply it to systems engineering

  1. List recurring problems that users or developers complain about.
  2. Identify which ones are avoided because they require difficult coordination or maintenance.
  3. Estimate their actual impact using evidence, not discomfort alone.
  4. Choose a small experiment that can test whether solving one would help.

Critical reading

The PDF describes cognitive filtering and claims that tedious work creates unusually large opportunities. These are strong generalizations, not experimental findings established by the document. A hard problem can also be hard because it has low demand, high cost, or no feasible solution. Use the idea as a hypothesis generator, not a universal rule.
