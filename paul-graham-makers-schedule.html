Paul Graham reading notes / Commentary

Maker's Schedule, Manager's Schedule

Reading notes based on the supplied PDF, pages 7–10. This is an independent interpretation, not the original essay.

Two ways to organize time

The PDF contrasts a manager's schedule, which often consists of shorter meetings and decisions, with a maker's schedule, which benefits from longer blocks for programming, writing, or research. The central concern is that a brief meeting can divide a useful work period into fragments.

Why interruptions matter

Complex engineering work often requires a mental model of code, assumptions, and constraints. Switching tasks may require rebuilding that context. The PDF gives vivid examples of this cost. Its exact estimates of lost time are illustrative claims, not measurements established by the archive.

Practical experiment

  1. Reserve one uninterrupted block for a difficult engineering task.
  2. Record what you completed and which interruptions occurred.
  3. On a comparable day, work with your usual meeting schedule.
  4. Compare progress and error rates. Do not assume one schedule always wins.

Working with a team

Batch nonurgent questions into agreed office hours or asynchronous updates when practical. Keep room for urgent incidents, collaboration, and other people's constraints. The goal is reliable output, not avoiding communication.
