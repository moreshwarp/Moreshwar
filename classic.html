EXP / 003 · REPLICATION● LIVE MODEL

What did your read actually see?

Write to one copy, pause delivery, and watch a second copy lag behind. It is a toy model of stale reads—not a consensus system.

→ → →updates
REPLICA / B0Latest delivered write

Manual delivery only. Demonstrates visible staleness; it does not implement quorum reads, leader election or agreement.

I / 01 — SYSTEM RELIABILITYSIMULATED / NOT MEASURED

If one backend is slow, how does fan-out change the odds?

Hypothesis
If independent downstream requests each have the same chance of being slow, making more requests increases the chance that at least one is slow.
What would challenge it?
Correlated latency, hedged requests, caching, or a different aggregation rule can make this simple model a poor predictor of real response time.
Next verifiable step
Run repeated client-side simulations and compare their measured frequency with the theoretical probability. Then vary correlations and identify the model's limits.
EXPERIMENT 01 / PROBABILITY LABANALYTICAL MODEL
P(AT LEAST ONE SLOW)33.7%

Illustrative probability: 1 − (1 − p)ⁿ. Assumes independent requests and that any slow call matters; this is not measured service telemetry.

03 / 2026 SCIENCE NOBEL PRIZES

From education to discovery.

Three short stories for future scientists: the question, the journey, and the lesson. Read the original sources before repeating a claim.

PHYSICS · CHEMISTRY · MEDICINE
2026 / PHYSICS↗
Francis Halzen

A telescope made from Antarctic ice

He helped pursue an unlikely idea: the clear ice deep below the South Pole might detect particles arriving from distant space. A vast instrument became a new way to study the universe.

THE LESSON

Start by testing whether an unusual material or environment can become a scientific instrument.

Education / path to discovery

Earned a master's degree and a PhD at the University of Louvain (now KU Leuven), Belgium.

Prize / discovery ↗ Education source ↗
2026 / CHEMISTRY↗
Henri B. Kagan & Kenso Soai

Why do molecules have a preferred handedness?

Their work explored how chemical reactions favor one mirror-image form of a molecule. Soai's research on self-amplifying reactions offers an especially striking example of how a small difference can grow.

THE LESSON

Small asymmetries can have large effects. Ask what mechanism amplifies them.

Education / path to discovery

Kenso Soai completed chemistry degrees at the University of Tokyo, earning his doctorate in 1979. This education note is about Soai, not Kagan.

Prize / discovery ↗ Education source ↗
2026 / PHYSIOLOGY OR MEDICINE↗
Karl Deisseroth, Peter Hegemann & Georg Nagel

A way to ask neurons questions using light

Research on light-sensitive proteins helped scientists develop optogenetics. With it, they could study the roles of particular nerve cells by controlling their activity with light.

THE LESSON

A basic discovery in one organism can help create powerful tools in another field.

Education / path to discovery

Karl Deisseroth studied biochemical sciences at Harvard and earned a neuroscience PhD and medical degree at Stanford. This education note is about Deisseroth, not his co-laureates.
